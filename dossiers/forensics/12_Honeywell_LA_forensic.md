## Headline / attribution split

This is the portfolio's primary shortfall case: **five consecutive performance years below guarantee (PY4–PY8)**, cumulative raw shortfall **−$84,325** as booked. Yet the shortfall dollars are not the real story — the real exposure is a **verification architecture that cannot substantiate its own numbers**: a 15-month BAS/historian data blackout (Aug 2023–Nov 6 2024) meant PY5, PY6, and PY7 Option-B ECMs were all reported on **prior-year substitute data**; six ECMs report savings "based off percent impact" with **no IPMVP calculation shown**; the ECM 5.1 lighting stream rests on Option-A stipulation with the **contractually required 2-site/5-year measurement never performed** since at least PY5; and NSS ECM 12.1 transformer savings are booked on **equipment that was never installed** (per Mod AS26).

Preliminary attribution of the −$84,325 raw shortfall and the disputed blocks:
- **Contractor-owed / recovered or make-good asserted: ~$54,403** — PY7 −$35,431 (recovered via P&I reduction, confirmed) + PY8 −$18,972 (make-good asserted, pending). High confidence these were handled correctly per ESPC doctrine.
- **Government-absorbed (reported, largely unchallenged in Matt-era-prior cycles): ~$154,927 of reported "actionable/mission" government cost impacts across PY5–PY6 (~$77,233 + ~$77,694)** plus the **$43,536 PY8 government-impact claim now formally challenged.** These are the memo-line government impacts Honeywell used to explain the gaps; whether they are legitimately government-responsible is exactly what GSA is now contesting.
- **Disputed / undetermined: the $43,536 PY8 claim (1,202 MMBtu)**, plus the entire pre-PY8 government-impact history, which was accepted without the causation/notification/trend substantiation GSA now demands (asymmetric scrutiny).
- **Unmeasured Option-A savings resting on stipulation:** full ECM 5.1 lighting savings stream (5 sites) + ECM 12.1 transformers — dollar magnitude of the annual lighting stream not isolated in records reviewed; flagged.

## 7. N. Spring St buyout — does the price account for the shortfall trend?

**Short answer: No, and it shouldn't directly — but two forensic hooks tie the buyout to this analysis.**

The buyout is a **debt-payoff / termination-value calculation**, not a performance valuation. Corrected government estimate ≈$2.87M (defensible range $1.9M–$2.9M): Termination Value $3,220,869 + fees + **2.5% premium** (§H.9) − refinancing credit, anchored to the 10/1/2026 TO-5A balance $11,560,653.93. The recurring shortfall trend does **not** enter this number — past shortfalls are handled separately via the annual §C.4.6 P&I reduction, and future performance risk transfers away with the removed building.

**Hook 1 — removed-savings basis (already caught):** the 8/13 COR briefing found the estimator mixed a gross TO-4 numerator with a net TO-1 denominator, overstating the removed-savings share at 27.86% vs the correct **24.10%** (~$434K impact). Getting the removed-savings share right is what keeps the buyout from over- or under-crediting the guarantee reduction. This is the buyout's real link to savings — the *forward* guarantee, not the *historical* shortfall.

**Hook 2 — NSS ECM 12.1 transformers (🔴):** NSS carries the ECM 12.1 transformer savings that were **never installed** (§9 above). The buyout allocation must **not pay Honeywell termination value premised on savings from equipment that never existed**, and GSA's parallel demand for a **permanent baseline adjustment for the NSS ECM 12.1 removals** should be reconciled *into* the buyout settlement (updated TO-1/TO-5A with restated guarantee) rather than left as a separate M&V action. If the buyout closes on the current savings base without excising the phantom transformer savings, GSA over-pays. **This is the one place where the shortfall/savings-integrity analysis directly threatens the buyout number** — flag for the proposal review (proposal due 8/25 12:00 PM CST; funds must obligate before 9/30/2026).
